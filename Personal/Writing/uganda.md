# A Khawaja’s Dream

*Writen on February 9, 2026\
Inspired by Dan Chiasson's poetry class: to turn a prose into a poem*

Seven days I press my feet into the cool concrete\
sneak under the bug nets you hung so carefully\
shielding us from blackout, malaria, drought,\
a storm drones up and down thinning walls.\
Baby K screams.\
We coo her, hold her, praise her custom gowns —\
What's her name? K for KimbAaly\
*Aaa* till your tongues raised.

Seven days our English takes on stubborn battles\
because I don’t speak Kakwa\
my Arabic never holds over a minute.\
You teach me again and again —\
*Anamingoni.*\
I repeat it over and over	till the van tumbles off.\
Chinese whispers		till it’s just sounds.\
I see you again? We will meet again? Nothing\
fetches my misplaced vowels and teases my tears\
when it soaks the back of your best shirt.

Seven days later, I am left with a souvenir \
elephant, more WhatsApp numbers than\
I can bring myself to text, and\
patterned fabric over my entire wall reminds me\
You never made my dress out of it.

Seven days and seven days, I find your voicemail\
with drunken swears in my inbox.\
sorry that your boss held you over the weekend, sorry\
missing you so much but never writing back\
arriving just to leave, flocking around\
self-conceited patrons with throwaway numbers.

Seven days and seven days and a new year, rain returns\
and persists to smooth the crackled roads.\
your day-long journey to the capital\
must be less bumpy, cassavas must be growing\
on your plates, boda bodas must be splashing\
around the streets and the Khawajas must leave

Would you believe I wish I stayed?

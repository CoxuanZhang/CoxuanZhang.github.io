# A Khawaja’s Dream

*Writen on February 9, 2026\
Inspired by Dan Chiasson's poetry class*

Seven days I press my feet into the cool concrete\
sneak under the bug nets you hung so carefully\
shielding us from blackout, malaria, drought,\
a storm so loud baby K cries in the other room.\
We coo her, hold her, praise her custom gowns —\
What's her name? not Maria with every\
A prolonged under raised tongues.

Seven days I stubbornly diverge from your harsh palatal English\
because I don’t speak Kakwa and\
my self introduction in Arabic never holds over a minute.\
You teach me again and again —\
*Anamingoni*.\ 
I repeat it over and over	till the van tumbles off.\
Chinese whispers till it’s just sounds.\
*I see you again? We will meet again?* Nothing around me\
finds my misplaced vowels and teases my tears\
when it soaks the back of your best shirt.

Seven days later, I am left with a souvenir\
elephant, more WhatsApp numbers than\
I can bring myself to text, and\
a long enough cloth to cover an entire wall\
because you never made my dress out of it.  

Seven days and seven days, I found your voicemail\
along with anonymous drunken swears in my +256 inbox.\
sorry that your boss held you over weekend\
sorry for missing you so much but never writing back\
sorry for arriving just to leave, flocking around as\
self-conceited patrons with throwaway numbers.

Seven days and seven days and a new year, rain returns\
and persists to smooth the crackled roads.\
your day-long journey to the capital must be less bumpy\
cassavas must be growing on your plates\
boda bodas must be roaming the streets\
and the Khawajas must leave,\
would you believe I wish I stayed?
